A disclosed violation is priced as a competence failure, not an integrity failure: trust moves from T to T·(1 − κ_c·s), with κ_c ≈ 0.15 and s the severity you assign. Full-severity disclosure costs about 15% of current trust; the same violation concealed and later discovered resets the account to its prior across every future deliverable. Disclosure is therefore the dominant strategy: it moves the event from the reset class to the recoverable class.

Recovery is slow by design: confirmed reliance gains in drops — η₊ ≈ 0.05 of the remaining gap per verifiable delivered claim — so a 15% loss takes several clean deliverables to rebuild. The recoverable rate applies only to honest disclosure: the severity field is the honesty field. Downgrading a core-contract violation to a "nuance" is misweighting, itself an integrity violation priced at reset.
