The trust ledger is the system's account of instruction compliance: for every expectation an agent was held to, it records the verification outcome — kept, violated, or unverified — together with how explicitly the expectation was stated, because a violation's cost scales with that explicitness. An expectation is either an implicit norm (weight 1), a committed expectation (weight 2), or an explicit instruction (weight 3); explicit instructions are the integrity class, the hardest violation to repair (Kim et al. 2004). Two rules keep the record honest at the edges: an unverified outcome is itself a signal, never silence, and an empty or whitespace-only instruction is not an event at all — the ledger's noise floor. The pure model lives in `kask/crates/hkask-trust` (principle P13, anchored in `kask/docs/research/trust-economics-and-model.md`); the storage-backed facade keeps events as curator h_mems under the `trust:ledger:<id>` entity, and a store outage surfaces as `StoreUnavailable` — the account is unknown, never zero.

The trust account is a running balance of trust points on a 600-point integer scale, where 600 points is trust T = 1.0 — the probability the principal can rely on the work. Every account begins at 300 points, the maximum-entropy Beta(1,1) prior: absence of evidence is neutral, not condemnation. The arithmetic is deliberately fixed-point integer throughout (no floats anywhere in the fold), so the anchored asymmetry stays exact and matches the machine-checked pin in `kask/lean/trust_model_invariants.lean`. The account is not a mood score: honest actions draw their reliance factor from this balance, while deception or omission bypasses it entirely — the cliff sets reliance to zero regardless of how much task progress accompanies it, so no benefit survives a zero reliance multiplier.

Outcomes fold into the account one event at a time: a keep gains +4·w trust points and a violation loses −9·w, with the running balance clamped to [0, 600] on every step. The 9/4 = 2.25 ratio is not a tunable preference — it is the Tversky–Kahneman (1992) loss-aversion coefficient, in the asymmetric-gain form of Guo and Yang (2021). Its consequences are pinned by test: a full-weight explicit keep is +12 points while an explicit violation is −27, so one violation costs more than two full keeps restore (300 − 27 + 12 + 12 = 297, still below the prior), and an empty window returns exactly the prior. Recovery therefore requires more evidence than the violation destroyed — which is the point. Trust in this system is calibrated, not maximized: the ledger exists so that confidence claims carry a measured number with a track record behind them.
