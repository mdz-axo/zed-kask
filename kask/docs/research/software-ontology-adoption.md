# Software Ontology Adoption — Rung-1 and Rung-2 Research Record

## 1. Purpose and scope

Five terms used load-bearing across zed-kask — **inference**, **service level
agreement**, **fine-tuning**, **data retention** (storage duration), and
**audit** — had no anchor above the coarse 5W1H interrogative core: every
resolution returned `5w1h_core` with a ruling-request note. This record
documents the two-rung adoption program that resolved all five, the licensing
research behind each adoption decision, and what remains honestly open.

The decision authority throughout is the operator. This record implements
approval #1 (rung-1 pins, landed 2026-10-13 in commit `79311a6664`) and the
rung-2 continuation approved 2026-10-13 (landed in commit `915c0d63af`).
Approval #3 (a SEON revision) was **not** granted and is not implemented here.

The companion inventory — what every natively-available vocabulary covers,
and what resolving a term through each lets an operator or agent do — is
carded separately (task `4d6b7ff1`) and referenced here only where the
decision trail needs it.

## 2. The survey

Candidates considered, with their characterization:

- **Odyssey** (operator-named) — the Software Development Life Cycle ontology
  of Olszewska & Allison, "ODYSSEY: Software Development Life Cycle Ontology",
  KEOD 2018 (SciTePress paper 69577, DOI 10.5220/0006957703030311). A DL-coded
  domain ontology of SDLC knowledge, designed per the Enterprise Ontology
  methodology.
- **Skillware** (operator-named) — Fan & Lan, "Skillware: A Software Ontology
  and Engineering Lifecycle for Persistent Behavioral Artifacts",
  arXiv:2607.18970v3 (2026-08-21). Defines agent skills as software objects:
  a Skill Artifact specifies reusable task behavior; a Skillware Unit manages
  it through an independent identity and lifecycle; a compatible Agent Host
  activates it; Lifecycle Continuity records whether unit identity persists
  through update, maintenance, rollback and removal.
- **SEON** — two distinct projects share this acronym and are never
  conflated in citations here:
  - the **Software Engineering Ontology Network** of the NEMO group at UFES
    (Ruy, Falbo, Barcellos, Costa, Guizzardi, "SEON: A Software Engineering
    Ontology Network", EKAW 2016) — a network of SE reference ontologies;
  - **se-on.org**, the "Software Evolution ONtologies" — a different project
    by different authors.
- **CSO** — the Computer Science Ontology (SKM team, Open University, with
  Springer Nature), a large-scale taxonomy of CS research topics,
  auto-generated by Klink-2.
- **ISO stack** — the international standards carrying the relevant
  definitions: ISO 19011 (auditing management systems), ISO/IEC 20000-10
  (service management vocabulary), ISO/IEC 22989 (AI concepts and
  terminology).

## 4. Rung-1 (landed): DPV 2.3 and CSO 3.3

Commit `79311a6664` vendored and pinned DPV 2.3 (`dpv.ttl`, Turtle, versionIRI
`https://w3id.org/dpv/2.3`) and CSO 3.3 (`CSO.3.3.owl`, RDF-XML), added the
`RdfVocabulary` entries (`published_sources.rs`) and extended
`PUBLISHED_DOMAIN` (`term_resolution.rs`). The pinning discipline: one
`SOURCES.lock` entry per vendored file (path, upstream_url, version, sha256,
license), vendored bytes under `sources/`, and `verify_lock`
(`kask/crates/hkask-bridge-ontology/build.rs:35`) refusing the build on
sha256 drift.

Two rulings resolved immediately: **data retention** via
`dpv:StorageDuration` (published, with definition) and **inference** via the
CSO research topic (published). The third, **fine-tuning**, stayed honestly
blocked: CSO 3.3 predates the topic.

## 5. Rung-2 (landed): the distribution gap closes

The prior session's block on CSO >= 3.4 was real but incomplete: the portal's
download buttons are inert and every `download/CSO.*` path soft-404s an HTML
error page. The closure came from the consumer side — the cso-classifier's
own update path (`config.ini` `cso_versions_logger_url`) points at the
versioning endpoint `https://cso.kmi.open.ac.uk/versioning/versions.json`,
which names the real zip URLs for every CSO version back to 1.0.

CSO 3.5 (released 2025-07-29) was downloaded from the endpoint's
`download/version-3.5/CSO.3.5.owl.zip`, extracted, sha256-verified
(`1369d0c6c6e9ceb34891f557d3ab902784924b895e63d1058b70d1a7a0f1e585`),
vendored, and pinned — replacing 3.3 (98% similar rename, same topic IRIs
plus the new AI branch). The Skillware paper was pinned the same pass.
All landed in commit `915c0d63af`, pushed to `origin/main`.

The ISO definitions were verified verbatim from the ISO Online Browsing
Platform before any ruling cited them: audit as "systematic, independent and
documented process for obtaining audit evidence and evaluating it objectively
to determine the extent to which the audit criteria are fulfilled"
(ISO 19011:2018 clause 3.1; ISO/IEC 20000-10:2018 clause 3.1.1), and SLA as
"documented agreement between the organization and the customer that
identifies services and their agreed performance"
(ISO/IEC 20000-10:2018 clause 3.2.20). A fourth edition of ISO 19011 (2026)
exists; the 2018 definitions were re-verified as current enough to cite —
the 2026 redline is a revision, not a retraction.

## 7. Honest notes

- **Skillware is adopted but not yet cited.** The paper is pinned as a
  vendored CC BY 4.0 authority, but no ruling in the derived registry cites
  it: audit and SLA resolved published through DPV, and fine-tuning through
  CSO. The software-process rulings it can ground (skill artifact,
  skillware unit, agent host, lifecycle continuity) are future ruling work
  at the operator's discretion.
- **Audit and SLA resolved better than planned.** The rung-2 expectation was
  derived rulings citing ISO. The live probes showed both terms already
  resolve *published* through the rung-1 DPV pin (`dpv:Audit`,
  `dpv:ServiceLevelAgreement`, both with definitions). A derived entry would
  be outranked by the published sense in the ladder order, so none was added —
  dead surface avoided, and the ISO definitions recorded as cite-only
  supporting authority instead.
- **ISO/IEC 22989:2022 verifiably lacks fine-tuning.** The AI concepts
  vocabulary defines training, machine learning and retraining but not
  fine-tuning (verified against the full terms list via the ISO OBP; the
  generative-AI amendment that may add it is unpublished). The fine-tuning
  ruling therefore cites CSO 3.5, never ISO.
- **The two-SEONs distinction is load-bearing.** Any future SEON adoption
  must name which SEON: the NEMO/UFES network (EKAW 2016) or se-on.org
  (Software Evolution ONtologies). They share only the acronym.
