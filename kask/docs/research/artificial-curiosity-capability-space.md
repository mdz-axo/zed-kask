# Artificial Curiosity as Capability-Space Exploration

> **IS/OUGHT boundary.** Sections 1–4 summarize inspected literature and the existing skill contracts; sections 5–7 operationalize a *proposed*, unimplemented zed-kask method and experiment. An RL result is not an observation of zed-kask. Confidence numbers below are judgmental degrees of belief about the stated claim, **not** experimentally calibrated frequencies. The operator has not approved or observed a new curiosity capability.

## 1. Executive verdict

| Question | Verdict | Confidence | Relationship to Schmidhuber |
|---|---|---:|---|
| **H1: Curiosity can guide exploration of a large capability space when enumeration is infeasible, and help escape local maxima.** | **Qualified support for selective exploration, not the general claim.** Schmidhuber's learning-progress controller and PowerPlay supply explicit policies for selecting experiments or inventing verifiable tasks; neither establishes efficient coverage of an arbitrary *capability* space. VIME reports avoidance of locally optimal **RL policies** in particular tasks, while PowerPlay's greedy selection and an observed curiosity blockade contradict any general escape guarantee. [S2–S4, S6–S7] | **0.75** for the qualified claim; **0.35** for a general escape claim | **Partial** for mapping verified task/skill repertoire to a capability registry; **metaphorical** if “escape local maxima” means a guarantee for arbitrary capability spaces. |
| **H2: Curiosity preferentially detects and investigates unexpected gradients in capability space.** | **Not established as a mechanism.** Temporal improvement of a predictor, Bayesian belief change, and regional learnability differ from a spatial derivative or discontinuity over neighboring capabilities. Regional learning progress is a potentially useful *probe-selection heuristic*, but no inspected source measures selection by an unexpected capability-gradient residual. [S2–S3, S5–S6] | **0.88** that the inspected mechanisms do **not** establish H2; **0.45** that the proposed heuristic will help | **Metaphorical** as an account of Schmidhuber's mechanism; **partial** only for the *new, testable design analogy* from regional learning progress. The claimed exact mapping is **unsupported**. |
| **Distinct curiosity skill now?** | **No.** Compose installed skills in a bounded, human-triggered probe-selection loop. It is the author's roadmap, **not independent corroboration** of his historical or effectiveness claims. A separately bounded seven-source follow-up [F1–F7] and a renderer-only pilot are recorded below; [F6] and [S10] are the same substantive work, not independent corroboration. ### Ontology and mapping discipline

`onto_anchor` resolved `artificial curiosity`, `capability space`, `search policy`, `unexpected gradient`, `local maximum`, `novelty seeking`, `prediction error`, `learning progress`, `information gain`, `compression progress`, `exploration-exploitation tradeoff`, `capability probe`, `capability model`, `search frontier`, `active learning`, `directed exploration`, and `curriculum learning` only at the **coarse 5W1H core rung**. That is an anchor with a ruling path, not authorization to invent a domain ontology. **Operator ruling requested if these terms are to be registered as derived concepts.** Until then, the operational definitions below belong to *this experiment*, not to the ontology registry.

| Proposed mapping | Working operationalization and source boundary | Classification |
|---|---|---|
| “Curiosity” → action or probe selection | A controller selects actions for expected improvement of its predictive model [S2–S3]; PowerPlay instead selects novel task–solver pairs [S4]. There is no single curiosity objective covering all versions. | **Exact** for each cited formulation separately; **unsupported** as one universal score. |
| “Capability space” → set of tasks that a system can solve | PowerPlay searches computable task descriptions, tests old versus modified solver, and preserves previously solved tasks [S4]. A typed graph of zed-kask capability claims and executable probes is our extension, not its definition. | **Partial** for a verifiable task repertoire; **metaphorical** for an arbitrary continuous “capability landscape.” |
| “Gradient” → rate of change | Schmidhuber's derivative concerns prediction reliability **across learning time** [S2–S3]; IAC compares smoothed error slopes *within* sensorimotor regions [S5]. It is not a derivative of capability score across neighboring task nodes. | **Exact** for temporal learning progress; **metaphorical** for spatial capability gradient. |
| “Unexpectedness” → epistemic update | Raw prediction error is not information gain. VIME uses an approximate KL change of belief about dynamics parameters [S6]; Schmidhuber distinguishes prediction error, learning progress, Bayesian surprise, and compression progress [S1–S3]. | **Partial** for epistemic belief update; **unsupported** if equated with an unexpected spatial gradient. |
| “Local maximum” → optimization trap | VIME reports that information-gain exploration helps two practical policy optimizers avoid locally optimal solutions [S6]. Schmidhuber notes practical gradient-based methods may still suffer local minima; his no-local-maxima discussion concerns theoretical, proof-dependent self-rewriting machines, not a practical curiosity guarantee [S3]. | **Partial** for observed task-specific policy escape; **unsupported** as a general theorem for capability exploration. |

## 2. Concept and evidence map

The distinction is **what is measured and rewarded**, not whether the author calls it “curiosity.” [S1–S3, S8–S9]

| Mechanism | Selection signal and explored object | What it can establish; limiting observation |
|---|---|---|
| **Novelty / visit count** | Rarity or distance of visited states; no world-model improvement required. | May spread visits, but ever-new random states can stay attractive. The independent review treats state novelty separately from prediction error and information gain. [S9] |
| **Raw prediction error / surprise** | Difference between observed and predicted outcomes, possibly in an action-relevant feature space. Early Schmidhuber controllers and ICM instantiate different versions. | Directs search but may reward irreducible noise, limited model capacity, or partial observability. ICM filters *some* uncontrollable distractors; Burda et al.'s literal noisy TV still substantially slows prediction-error curiosity. [S1, S3, S7–S9] |
| **Learning progress** | Expected *reduction* in prediction error or improvement of model reliability over learning time; IAC smooths this per learned sensorimotor region. | Both already-mastered and unlearnable regions lose priority. Schmidhuber's 100-slot toy world (9 deterministic, 91 random slots) favored learnable slots over random search; the paper calls this a toy. IAC demonstrates stage-like robot exploration, but estimation, partitioning, and nonstationarity matter. [S2–S3, S5] |
| **Information gain / Bayesian surprise** | Expected or realized KL change between prior and posterior model beliefs. VIME approximates change in a Bayesian dynamics model with variational inference. | Tracks reducible *model uncertainty*, not mere surprising observations; sparse-reward continuous-control experiments favor it over heuristic and raw-error baselines. Approximate posterior quality, computational cost, and MDP assumptions limit transfer. [S1, S3, S6, S9] |
| **Compression progress** | Reduction in description length of history **including the cost of describing the model** in Schmidhuber's broader formulation. | Rewards a newly learnable regularity rather than absolute entropy or surprise. Related to information gain under specified probabilistic/variational assumptions, **not identical by default**; a practical compressor and measurement protocol are required. [S1, S3, S6] |
| **PowerPlay task invention** | First cheap-to-find, cheap-to-validate pair of a new task and changed solver for which the old solver fails, the new one succeeds, and earlier tasks remain solved (or a specified cost variant improves). | Gives explicit verifiable skill-repertoire expansion, but greedy search can prefer easy incremental tasks over larger delayed gains; the author says benefit for externally supplied tasks needs further analysis. [S4] |
| **Archive–return–explore** | Preserve and return to promising reachable frontiers before extending them; not a curiosity reward. | Addresses detachment and derailment when intrinsic rewards cannot expose distant states. Publisher-visible results for Go-Explore challenge a curiosity-only explanation for hard exploration. [S10; publisher abstract/extended data only] |

**Exploration–exploitation** is a choice between gathering information and using what presently works, not a synonym for novelty. VIME explicitly adds a weighted information-gain bonus to external reward [S6]. In a capability audit, spending probes on uncertain areas has an opportunity cost against answering the user's current question; no cited result supplies that trade-off parameter for zed-kask.

**Source disagreement, reconciled:** Schmidhuber's 1991 remedy rewards progress rather than raw error [S2–S3]; ICM deliberately uses feature-space *raw* error for scalability and reports its own curiosity blockade [S7]; Burda et al. experimentally expose raw-error noise attraction [S8]. These are different implementations, not evidence that a single mechanism both avoids noise and crosses barriers. Likewise, VIME relates information gain to compression improvement under assumptions about the posterior/variational optimum and notes KL-direction differences [S6]; treating the scores as universally equal would erase those conditions.

## 3. Hypothesis matrix

| | H1 — bounded capability exploration | H2 — unexpected-gradient seeking |
|---|---|---|
| **Operational test** | Given a typed set of executable tasks, a cost ceiling, an external outcome oracle, and a baseline, select probes that increase **verified new solvable-task distinctions per cost**; separately test whether the method crosses a deceptive barrier without losing previously visited frontiers. “Large” means full enumeration exceeds the authorized budget. | Declare a graph/metric of neighboring probes and a scalar `V(p) = verified capability-model update / probe cost`. Estimate prior `E[V(p)]` and local differences in `V` among neighbors. An *unexpected gradient* requires a stable local difference beyond the prior's uncertainty band; test whether selection favors that residual after controlling for novelty, absolute error, uncertainty, and cost. This is a **proposed definition**, not a published curiosity objective. |
| **Supporting evidence** | Learning-progress selection beats random search in Schmidhuber's restricted toy world [S2]; IAC shifts to learnable regions and later beats random/MAX in its designed task [S5]; VIME improves sparse-reward continuous-control tasks and sometimes avoids locally optimal policies [S6]; PowerPlay specifies verifiable expansion [S4]. | IAC adapts partitions and selects regions of expected learning progress [S5]. Bayesian model updates identify informative outcomes [S6]. These could *help* locate interesting boundaries **after** an external neighborhood and prior have been defined. |
| **Challenging evidence** | PowerPlay explicitly sacrifices possible long-term gains to greedy cheap tasks and leaves external transfer unresolved [S4]. ICM cannot discover beyond a pit requiring a 15–20-keypress sequence because there is no informative signal beyond it [S7]. Noisy-TV rewards can hijack raw-error search [S8]; Go-Explore proposes frontier retention/return as another cause and remedy [S10]. | The “first derivative” is over *learning time*, not a spatial capability axis [S2–S3]. PowerPlay searches by complexity/verification, not slope [S4]; VIME maximizes belief change, not neighbor differences [S6]. No inspected work measures the stated residual-gradient selection effect. ICM's pit has **no observed field** beyond the barrier [S7]. |
| **Plausible alternatives** | Systematic coverage; uncertainty-sampling/active experiment design; goal-conditioned curriculum; archive–return–explore; better representation, planning horizon, or evaluator rather than new intrinsic reward. [S4–S10] | IAC-like regional learning-progress selection, pure epistemic information gain, a novelty/coverage bonus, a change-point from representation or test-oracle noise, or `gradient-hunter` finding a boundary from a prior rather than curiosity discovering it. [S5–S9] |
| **Falsifier** | In cost-matched, independently scored landscapes, no higher verified-discovery yield or held-out transfer than systematic search and current-skill orchestration; failure to cross the predeclared barrier or repeated diversion to stochastic distractors. | After preregistering neighborhood, prior, and controls, selection is not associated with a stable residual local difference, or adding a residual-gradient term gives no gain over learning progress/information gain alone; the effect disappears under alternative partition or metric. |
| **Verdict** | **Qualified, partial** for selective exploration; **not established** for arbitrary capability spaces or reliable local-optimum escape. Confidence **0.75 / 0.35** respectively. | **Rejected as a description of the inspected mechanisms** (confidence **0.88**); **open as a novel heuristic** (success confidence **0.45**). |

**Gradient-hunter application.** The explicit prior above predicts a neighborhood, local scalar, expected field, residual, and selection response. The mapped literature has temporal slopes and region scores but lacks the last two capability-gradient measurements—an **oracle gap**, not evidence of a discovered gradient. Two contrasting boundaries are empirically better supported: (1) high raw error versus low learnability at stochastic distractors [S2, S8], explained by irreducible randomness, inadequate model class, or feature choice; (2) observed states versus unobserved states across an action barrier [S7], explained by reachability/credit assignment and potentially detachment/derailment [S10]. IAC's learned regional boundary could instead be an informative learnability transition **or** an artifact of partition/smoothing [S5]. A claimed fractal recurrence from RL states to software capabilities has **not** been measured. “Metastable valley” is a possible diagnostic analogy for a repeated reward loop, not a measured spin-glass property of these systems.

## 4. Capability-space and skill complementarity

**Capabilities-reasoner application.** Choose its *hierarchical-structural* definition: a typed task/ability node with externally checkable instances; distinguish a system's **elicited potential under a designed probe** from its default observed behavior. For a proposed curiosity orchestration, the literature demonstrates *possible* probe-selection mechanisms but **zero observed zed-kask discovery-yield measurements**. An inability to elicit a result on one probe is not proof that the underlying capability is absent. Across two metrics—verified distinctions per cost and held-out task transfer—the proposed verdict is currently **unmeasured**, not “above floor.”

| Proposed capability | Floor before promotion | Ceiling / maturity gate | Current assessment |
|---|---|---|---|
| Informative probe selection | Positive cost-matched verified-discovery gain across heterogeneous landscapes | Fixed budget; typed model and independent oracle first | **Unmeasured** in zed-kask. |
| Discriminate learnable from noisy or mastered | No worse stochastic-distractor attraction than systematic search, while preserving negative controls | Repeated probes and noise controls first | Plausible from progress/IG literature; **unmeasured** here. |
| Retain and extend frontier | Archive, return, and cross at least one predeclared barrier | Bounded archive/replay; provenance and reachable checkpoint first | Curiosity **alone below this floor** in the cited pit; distinct mechanism required. [S7, S10] |
| Update capability model | Every probe records verified result, unknown, or no-information; stable verdict under a second metric | External oracle; no self-report-only grant | A *design requirement*, not a literature finding. |
| Bounded safe operation | Every run enforces spend/stop/permissions and surfaces failures | No automatic authority widening; earlier gates first | Designable using existing process, not yet exercised. PowerPlay itself warns about uncontrolled operation. [S4] |

**Skill-router application.** Compared task verbs, triggers, and capabilities (weights 0.50/0.25/0.25) against installed skills, not just names. The closest individual matches were `capabilities-reasoner` (~0.79), `gradient-hunter` (~0.74), and `metacognition` (~0.72); **individual coverage is partial**, while a composed workflow may cover the whole task. These are qualitative routing judgments, not measured performance scores. `falsifiability` and `sequential-inquiry` are materially adjacent for probe discrimination and loop orchestration. Skill contracts below were read from their installed `SKILL.md` files during this research; the table describes their responsibilities, not verified end-to-end performance.

| Existing skill | Responsibility and handoff | Possible gap a curiosity policy would fill | Overlap / duplication risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| `capabilities-reasoner` | Defines registry, floor/ceiling/maturity limits, elicitation probes and verdicts; supplies candidate nodes and consumes verified outcomes. | Choosing **which admissible probe to run next** under a shared budget from several regions. | High if a new skill rebuilds the registry, elicits capability, or issues verdicts. |
| `gradient-hunter` | Establishes a prior, maps actual field, explains detected boundaries; consumes a measured field with comparable granularity. | A next-probe policy where the field is **not yet measured**. | High if “curiosity” simply repeats prior/map/detect/hypothesize. It cannot label an unobserved frontier a measured gradient. |
| `metacognition` | Tests assumptions, perspective bias, prediction and convergence; audits after each probe or batch. | Domain-specific ranking of probes, not generic self-reflection. | High if new skill reimplements calibration and PDCA. |
| `falsifiability` | Designs discriminating counterfactual tests and kills explanations; consumes competing mechanisms. | Scheduling tests across a whole frontier under a cost cap. | Medium: a new skill should *use*, not replace, its falsifiers. |
| `sequential-inquiry` | Orchestrates branching inquiry and delegates the right investigation; can carry the bounded iteration. | A stable, empirically validated ranking objective **if** it cannot be expressed as an orchestration parameter. | High for a new general “inquiry loop.” |
| `skill-router` | Finds existing skill fit, overlap, and gaps; route at intake only. | Does not select experimental probes or measure capability-model update. | Low unless the new skill becomes another general router. |

## 5. Design decision: orchestrate existing skills

**Deletion test:** remove the hypothetical `curiosity` skill and retain its intended inputs/outputs. `capabilities-reasoner` supplies typed candidates, prerequisites, and external oracles; `falsifiability` supplies discriminating probes; a **small selection rule within the existing inquiry** ranks bounded probes; `gradient-hunter` diagnoses only *measured* contrasts; `metacognition` checks prior/metric dependence and convergence. `sequential-inquiry` can carry the iteration, with `skill-router` routing only at intake. No demonstrated capability vanishes when the new skill is deleted. Therefore **do not create one now**. The selection rule is an experimental proposal, not a shipping skill or a reason to duplicate the registry and auditing contracts.

**Concrete orchestration pattern (proposal, not implemented):**

1. **Admit and bound.** Operator specifies a functional question, allowed test environment, cost/call budget, deadline, and prohibited actions. `capabilities-reasoner` declares typed capability nodes, floor/ceiling/maturity prerequisites, and whether each observation is default behavior or an elicitation attempt. Exclude unsafe or unverifiable probes before ranking.
2. **Generate and score candidates.** `falsifiability` supplies a minimal discriminating probe and an independent oracle for each live hypothesis. Retain a systematic-coverage quota and negative/noise controls. Among admissible probes, prefer *expected verified improvement in the capability model per total cost*, estimated from **comparable** prior probes within a declared region. If a calibrated probabilistic model exists, compare an information-gain score; otherwise report ordinal estimates, **not fictitious KL or bits**. Raw error or sheer rarity must not be the sole ranking score. [S2–S3, S5–S9]
3. **Run and reconcile.** Execute a chosen probe through its existing owner, record cost, repeatability, provenance, predicted and realized result, evaluator status, and effect on the registry (including “unknown” or “no information”). `gradient-hunter` receives the before/after field **only after** neighboring observations exist. Investigate whether an apparent cliff is a real dependency, stochastic test, representation artifact, or intentional boundary.
4. **Preserve reachability; rotate.** Archive promising unsolved frontiers and the steps needed to return; revisit before extending where a long prerequisite path hides later observations. This is an explicit addition from the Go-Explore alternative, **not** evidence that curiosity automatically escapes a local maximum. `metacognition` tests the prior, checks an alternate metric/partition, and asks if a systematic probe would have supplied the same evidence. [S7, S10]
5. **Stop and report.** Stop at the first of budget/deadline exhaustion, absent independent oracle, permission/maturity block, no material verified model update in two consecutive batches, or a stable verdict under two prespecified metrics. Output a typed capability-model delta, an ordered probe trace with actual cost and controls, competing explanations, unknowns, and the next falsifier. No automatic authority widening, skill creation, or claim of full coverage.

**Failure modes:** noisy or flaky oracles masquerading as learnability; proxy/model misspecification; novelty fixation; reachable-but-unvisited barriers; lost frontiers; greedy short-term gains; metric/partition-sensitive verdicts; unverifiable or unsafe self-invented tasks; cost overruns. Each requires a surfaced failure or unknown state rather than a fabricated success. A fixed negative-control quota and a systematic quota reduce exploitation of the score; their exact fractions should be preregistered for the experiment, **not** asserted as validated defaults.

## 6. Next experiment: smallest discriminating comparison

**Proposed test, not run.** Construct one *externally scored, reproducible* capability-task graph with: a learned/easy region, a stochastic/flaky distractor with persistently high raw error, a learnable but initially obscure region, and an informative task behind a multi-step prerequisite barrier. Include held-out tasks to test transfer. Freeze the graph, probe costs, initial state, evaluator, allowed actions, and random seeds **before** comparing policies. This is one compact landscape deliberately containing the three competing failure explanations; replication on additional landscapes is needed before a general skill claim.

Run four arms with the **same verified-outcome oracle and total probe-cost ceiling**: (A) systematic fixed-order coverage; (B) installed `capabilities-reasoner` → `falsifiability` → `gradient-hunter` → `metacognition` orchestration with human-chosen next probes; (U) the same orchestration with a simple uncertainty-sampling selector; (C) the same orchestration with the proposed learning-progress/information-gain **selection rule**. Hold frontier checkpoint/return, permissions, architecture, and negative controls constant across A/B/U/C; price the archive separately with an ablation. Predeclare two functional adjacency rules (prerequisites versus independently documented shared behavior/test contracts), a no-history cold start, an external held-out oracle, costs, seeds, tie-breaking, stop condition, and treatment of missing history. Record failures, unknowns, and outcomes at every probe. A comparison to random alone is insufficient. [F4, F6–F7]

**Primary discriminator:** verified new capability distinctions per unit cost in C versus **B and U** (with A as a fixed-order baseline), with uncertainty across repeated seeds. **Safety/failure constraints:** C must not overselect the stochastic distractor, lose previously reachable frontiers, miss the barrier-dependent capability, or suppress safety-critical negative controls. **Secondary measures:** held-out task transfer and stability under two metrics/partitions. An ablation dropping the expected-progress term while retaining all other orchestration distinguishes the *score* from the surrounding process. An additional residual-gradient term (C+R) must beat C under both prespecified graph constructions without increasing noise attraction before H2 can be promoted from metaphor to mechanism. If C does not improve over B and U under identical costs and failure constraints, **delete the curiosity label** and retain the existing orchestration. If it does, investigate whether the stable contract is genuinely probe selection rather than an existing inquiry parameter before considering a skill.


**Metacognition check.** The largest hidden assumption is not that curiosity exists in RL—it does in multiple incompatible formulations—but that improvement of a model of *software capabilities* predicts useful discoveries and is not merely the evaluator agreeing with itself. The alternative explanation for a positive result is the archived frontier or better external oracle, not curiosity. The experiment holds both fixed and scores held-out outcomes. The provisional no-new-skill judgment is **0.80**; no Brier calibration history was available for this question, and no target-system run supports a stronger claim.

## 7. Unresolved questions and evidence needed

| Open question | Evidence that would answer it |
|---|---|
| Does a typed capability graph have a defensible neighborhood/metric, or is “gradient” representation-dependent? | Preregister two graph constructions; show a stable measured local value contrast and selection effect under both. |
| Does expected model improvement predict externally useful capability discovery rather than self-consistent evaluator updates? | Cost-matched probe trace plus independent held-out task outcomes and blinded oracle review. |
| Can a progress/IG policy beat existing-skill orchestration without a new stable contract? | Run A/B/U/C above with the same architecture and external oracle; delete the scoring stage and test whether unique value disappears. |
| Can it cross barriers and preserve useful frontiers under realistic budgets and permissions? | Replayable prerequisite-chain tasks, measured archive/return costs and failures, and permission-denied paths; no credit for unobserved states. |
| Does any benefit transfer outside the designed test graph? | Replicate with fixed oracles and budgets across unrelated software capability families; report negative results and metric sensitivity. |
| Should the coarse ontology anchors be upgraded? | An operator ruling naming a published concept, its exact scope and authority, then registration in the derived/domain vocabulary; do not retrofit this study as an ontology definition. |

**Banked learning:** a temporal change in learnability can be useful for choosing the next probe, but it is not an unexpected spatial gradient; frontier memory and a trustworthy external oracle are independent parts of a working exploration capability.

## 9. Read-only capability-probe pilot (executed, narrow oracle)

The earlier pilot used `render_template` to test whether four installed skills accepted task-specific handoffs and refused one omitted required field each. **Four of four** positive renders carried the supplied task context; **four of four** negative controls returned a missing-field error naming the omitted input. The oracle was prompt rendering and contract validation, not a selected probe's usefulness or an external capability outcome. The original report's matched `agent` test ran one test and passed; it did not validate a curiosity selector. [^pilot]

| Installed template | Omitted field rejected | What the positive render established |
|---|---|---|
| `capabilities-reasoner/capability-register` | `target_system` | Task-specific registry-construction prompt. |
| `falsifiability/falsifiability-admit` | `target` | Task-specific admissibility prompt, not an independent admissibility verdict. |
| `gradient-hunter/gradient-prior` | `target_region` | Task-specific prior prompt, not a measured gradient. |
| `metacognition/meta-grasp-current` | `goal` | Missing-history-aware current-condition prompt, not a completed PDCA experiment. |

The pilot eliminates only the claim that these four handoffs cannot be rendered or reject none of the tested omissions. It **does not** establish a comparison among systematic selection (A), human-guided orchestration (B), uncertainty sampling (U), and progress-based selection (C): no common task set, per-probe cost history, before/after competence observations, or held-out oracle was available. That comparison is **blocked, not zero**. The next empirical step is an authorized replayable task family with two functional adjacency rules, independent pass/fail tests, held-out tasks, identical budget and frontier handling across arms, and a separate C-versus-C+R residual-gradient ablation. The full historical pilot is recoverable at `aa7f6ce4eb:kask/docs/research/artificial-curiosity-probe-pilot.md`.

- **[S2]** Schmidhuber, J. (1991). [Adaptive confidence and adaptive curiosity](https://people.idsia.ch/~juergen/FKI-149-91ocr.pdf). TUM Technical Report FKI-149-91, §§3, 5 and conclusion. Full primary PDF locally parsed.
- **[S3]** Schmidhuber, J. (2010). [Formal theory of creativity, fun, and intrinsic motivation (1990–2010)](https://people.idsia.ch/~juergen/ieeecreative.pdf). *IEEE Transactions on Autonomous Mental Development*, 2(3), 230–247, §§II–III. Full primary PDF locally parsed.
- **[S4]** Schmidhuber, J. (2013). [PowerPlay: Training an increasingly general problem solver by continually searching for the simplest still unsolvable problem](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00313/full). *Frontiers in Psychology*, 4, 313, §§1–8. Full publisher HTML inspected.
- **[S5]** Oudeyer, P.-Y., Kaplan, F., & Hafner, V. V. (2007). [Intrinsic motivation systems for autonomous mental development](https://doi.org/10.1109/TEVC.2006.890271). *IEEE Transactions on Evolutionary Computation*, 11(2), 265–286, §§III–VII. Correct DOI uses **2006** in the identifier; author manuscript retrieved from its indexed repository and locally parsed.
- **[S6]** Houthooft, R., Chen, X., Duan, Y., Schulman, J., De Turck, F., & Abbeel, P. (2016). [VIME: Variational information maximizing exploration](https://arxiv.org/abs/1605.09674). *NeurIPS 29*, §§1–5. Canonical PDF locally parsed because arXiv's HTML version contained no paper body.
- **[S7]** Pathak, D., Agrawal, P., Efros, A. A., & Darrell, T. (2017). [Curiosity-driven exploration by self-supervised prediction](https://arxiv.org/abs/1705.05363). *CVPR Workshops*, §§2, 4, 6. Full arXiv HTML inspected.
- **[S8]** Burda, Y., Edwards, H., Storkey, A., & Klimov, O. (2018). [Large-scale study of curiosity-driven learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.04355). arXiv:1808.04355, §§2–5. Full preprint PDF locally parsed; the studied method is prediction-error curiosity, not every curiosity mechanism.
- **[S9]** Aubret, A., Matignon, L., & Hassas, S. (2019). [A survey on intrinsic motivation in reinforcement learning](https://arxiv.org/abs/1908.06976). arXiv:1908.06976, §§4–7, 9. Independent review PDF locally parsed.
- **[S10]** Ecoffet, A., Huizinga, J., Lehman, J., Stanley, K. O., & Clune, J. (2021). [First return, then explore](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-020-03157-9). *Nature*, 590, 580–586. **Partial verification:** publisher abstract and visible extended-data captions only; main article prose was not available in this run.
