# Artificial Curiosity: Open-Questions Follow-up

> **IS/OUGHT boundary.** This addendum follows [Artificial Curiosity as Capability-Space Exploration](artificial-curiosity-capability-space.md) §7. Literature findings below are observations *in the cited experiments*, not zed-kask results. The proposed evaluation is a protocol, **not a completed run**. The original ten-source corpus remains bounded as originally specified; this is a separate seven-source follow-up. Research ledger `8e5ebaf54b494313` records source provenance. Six full works were inspected (five primary experimental papers plus a NeurIPS paper); [F7] was inspected at abstract level only. The two Go-Explore URLs refer to a previously counted work, not independent replication.

## Decision update

**Continue with existing-skill orchestration; do not create a curiosity skill.** The new evidence strengthens the need for an explicit representation, an independently scored outcome, and a frontier-return control. It does *not* show a spatial “unexpected gradient” is invariant to how tasks are represented, or that a curiosity score uniquely improves software-capability discovery. Stronger evidence for modular competence-progress selection changes the priority of testing the score, not the deletion-test decision. [F1–F6]

## Open-question ledger

| Original §7 question | What the follow-up establishes | State; what remains |
|---|---|---|
| **Is a typed capability neighborhood defensible, or is the gradient representation-dependent?** | Péré et al. vary learned representation family, latent dimension and goal sampling in simulated tasks: exploration coverage changes, including underperformance by a radial-flow variational autoencoder (RFVAE) and boundary-seeking effects when goal sampling extends beyond reachable outcomes. A follow-up with simulated *and physical* robot arms compares learned and engineered goal spaces; learned representations can match or outperform baselines, but it does not prove invariance across representations. Cully et al. use a **user-defined** behavioral descriptor map; measured real-world performance updates nearby behaviors through a Gaussian process. Thus adjacency is useful *conditional on* a chosen representation, not discovered as a canonical capability metric. [F1–F3] | **Narrowed, not closed.** Preregister two typed zed-kask graphs with different, defensible neighbor rules (e.g. shared test/behavioral contract vs prerequisite/transition edge), then compare the sign and ordering of *externally measured* local contrasts under both. If a contrast flips, call it representation-sensitive, not a discovered gradient. A text-embedding distance alone does not establish functional adjacency. |
| **Does expected model improvement predict externally useful discovery, not just self-consistent updates?** | CURIOUS estimates *module competence* using separate evaluation episodes and assesses success on experimenter-defined achievable goals using offline evaluation rollouts not used for training. Its modular architecture improves on flat-policy baselines; subsequent comparisons to the **same modular UVFA without progress-based selection** show faster recovery following a time-locked sensor perturbation and increasing advantage with distractor modules. This distinguishes the selection policy from some, but not all, architectural effects. Lopes et al. study empirical prediction accuracy/progress under restricted model-learning assumptions and nonstationarity; neither paper measures software-capability discovery or general held-out transfer. [F4–F5] | **Narrowed, not closed.** Keep the probe selector's model-update score distinct from an independently defined target: held-out pass/fail task outcomes, externally reviewed evidence, and task transfer. Fix the representation, evaluator, policy architecture, budget, and frontier behavior when ablating the score. If internal score rises without held-out improvement, reject the proxy. |
| **Does a progress/information-gain policy beat existing-skill orchestration without its own contract?** | CURIOUS's cleanest contrast is a progress-guided vs random module selection within a modular architecture; it reports robustness to unrealisable distracting modules and quicker recovery. It does not compare against zed-kask's human-guided orchestration. A 2025 active-learning benchmark reports in its abstract that compatible uncertainty sampling remains competitive and query/learner model incompatibility can degrade it; that comparison concerns *tabular classification*, not capability audits. [F4, F7] | **Open.** Compare systematic selection, the existing skill composition with human-chosen probes, the *same* composition with uncertainty sampling, and the *same* composition with learning-progress/expected-information-gain selection. A curiosity win against random alone is insufficient; preserve cost and oracle parity, and include the strongest simple query baseline. |
| **Can it cross barriers and preserve frontiers within budgets and permissions?** | The complete Go-Explore preprint distinguishes archiving promising states, returning to them, and then exploring. Its reported cell-discovery improvement from a learned goal-conditioned policy over random post-return actions depends on an established exploration/return setup; state representation and simulator restoration are significant affordances. Cully et al. instead seed real-world trials with a simulation-built behavior-performance map and update it from measured outcomes. Neither demonstrates permission-safe restoration of arbitrary zed-kask tasks. [F3, F6] | **Narrowed, not closed.** Define *reachable* checkpoints and explicit prerequisites in an authorized sandbox. Measure restoration failures, checkpoint size, return effort, and discoveries beyond the barrier for both arms while holding the archive fixed. Never attribute an archive/return gain to curiosity scoring. Never infer a gradient in an unobserved region. |
| **Does benefit transfer outside a designed graph?** | Péré et al. test two simulated task families; the goal-space follow-up also runs a physical robot, but warns that its robot lacks distractors and that online representation training used only initial observations. CURIOUS tests a modular-goal robotic environment with controlled distractors and perturbation. Cully et al. test different robot damage conditions and behavioral characterizations. These add robustness checks *within robotics*, not independent software families. [F1–F4] | **Open.** After a pilot, freeze policies and assess separate software families with different probe costs, oracles, and prerequisites; publish failures and representation-sensitive reversals. Do not use the same synthetic graph for tuning and held-out evidence. |
| **Should ontology anchors be upgraded?** | Papers define different *study-specific* spaces—latent goal/outcome, module/competence, behavior descriptor, archive cell. None supplies an authoritative, shared definition of “capability-space gradient.” [F1–F4, F6] | **Open; operator ruling required.** `onto_anchor` again returned only coarse `5w1h_core` for `capability probe neighborhood`, `expected information gain`, `held-out task transfer`, and `adaptive experiment design`. No ontology registry was changed. A derived/domain registration needs an operator-selected published authority and a precise scope. |

### A concrete alternative to a spatial gradient

Cully et al. offer the strongest adjacent *operational* pattern: precompute a map indexed by explicit behavior descriptors; assign low confidence to simulated predictions in the real world; select a trial with an acquisition function that balances predicted performance and uncertainty; update tested and neighboring descriptors with **measured** robot performance. That is a prior-informed experimental-design loop, not an agent preferentially finding an *unexpected derivative* of capability across an intrinsically given space. Its descriptor robustness check does not remove its dependence on designer-chosen axes or simulation quality. [F3]

CURIOUS supplies a different pattern: estimate changes in *externally testable goal competence* per module, including deterioration, and direct further practice accordingly. This is a temporal slope **within modules**. Treating a contrast between modules as an unexpected *spatial* gradient adds an untested adjacency and prior-residual layer. [F4]

## Discriminating experiment protocol (not executed)

**Fixed functional target:** find and verify previously unknown task capabilities at less authorized cost, without hiding negatives, overselecting noise, or losing a reachable frontier. The operator must approve the allowed test environment, target families, costs and oracle; no autonomous authority widening. The first pilot tests that the protocol can distinguish methods, not that a new skill has been validated.

1. **Predeclare two representations and the external oracle.** Construct a task graph based on explicit prerequisites and a second graph based on independently documented shared behavior/test contracts. Register probe-to-capability mapping *before* results; an expert independent of the selector defines the pass/fail task oracle and holds back transfer tasks. Reject a graph whose edges are mere similarity labels without a functional justification. For each run, distinguish tasks unreachable under permissions from tasks reachable but unseen.
2. **Freeze common resources.** Identical probe candidates, observations, action permissions, budget accounting, restart/checkpoint rules, negative controls and evaluator for all arms. Include a solvable region, an already-known region, an unreachable goal, a noisy/flaky test oracle, and a reachable task behind a multistep prerequisite. Report failures, unknowns and costs as outcomes. Preregister scoring formulas, tie-breaking, seeds, stopping rule and a no-history cold start; do **not** claim numeric KL where no probabilistic posterior has been validated.
3. **Factor the intervention.** Arm A: systematic fixed-order probes. B: `capabilities-reasoner` + `falsifiability` + `gradient-hunter` + `metacognition`, with human choice of next probe. U: B with a simple uncertainty-sampling selector. C: B with an expected verified competence/model-improvement-per-total-cost selector. Use the *same* frontier archive/return in A/B/U/C, and a separate **archive ablation** only to price its effect. For H2 only, compare C with C+R, where R is a prespecified residual-local-contrast term on the same graph. No architectural advantage may be credited to the score. [F4, F6–F7]
4. **Measure externally and across representations.** Primary outcome: independent, verified new task distinctions per actual cost **versus B and U**, reported over repeated seeds and as a time/cost curve. Secondary: held-out task accuracy/transfer, barrier-dependent discovery, noise allocation, checkpoint-return cost and permission violations. Log predicted score, realized update, oracle provenance and graph choice *for every probe*. Blind the held-out judge to arm identity. A claimed gradient requires C+R to beat C under both prespecified representations without increased noise attraction; a positive score effect without transfer remains a proxy-only result.
5. **Make the deletion decision.** No gain over B and U, proxy-only gain, or failure of safety/representation checks → retain orchestration and delete the curiosity-specific score. Gain over B/U under fixed costs, stable external oracle and independent families → investigate whether the stable new contract is *selection* rather than a new registry, audit, or general reasoning loop; only then reconsider a new skill.

**Not run:** no graph, task outcomes, repetitions, cost curves, or new skill exist in this follow-up. The four-arm comparison is more discriminating than the original three-arm test because uncertainty sampling is a credible simple alternative, and the archive is held constant. The report does **not** revise the original H1/H2 confidence figures based on unrelated domains.

## Evidence and provenance

The seven works below are the **follow-up** evidence set, not additions to the original ten-source synthesis. [F6] is the full author preprint of the original report's partially accessible [S10], not an independent corroborating study. [F7] is abstract-only. Bibliographies inside these works were not themselves used as evidence.

- **[F1]** Péré, A., Forestier, S., Sigaud, O., & Oudeyer, P.-Y. (2018). [Unsupervised learning of goal spaces for intrinsically motivated goal exploration](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.00781). ICLR. Full PDF: §4 and figures 2–4 on representation and latent dimension.
- **[F2]** Laversanne-Finot, A., Péré, A., & Oudeyer, P.-Y. (2021). [Intrinsically motivated exploration of learned goal spaces](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/neurorobotics/articles/10.3389/fnbot.2020.555271/full). *Frontiers in Neurorobotics*, 14, 555271. Full HTML: §§2–4, 6; extends [F1] to a physical robot, **not independent authorship/replication**.
- **[F3]** Cully, A., Clune, J., Tarapore, D., & Mouret, J.-B. (2015). [Robots that can adapt like animals](https://arxiv.org/abs/1407.3501). *Nature*, 521, 503–507. Full author preprint: behavior map, robot trials and methods; an adjacent alternative to curiosity.
- **[F4]** Colas, C., Fournier, P., Sigaud, O., Chetouani, M., & Oudeyer, P.-Y. (2019). [CURIOUS: Intrinsically motivated modular multi-goal reinforcement learning](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v97/colas19a.html). ICML/PMLR 97, 1331–1340. Full PDF: §§2.4, 4.1–4.4. Separates module architecture and selection comparisons.
- **[F5]** Lopes, M., Lang, T., Toussaint, M., & Oudeyer, P.-Y. (2012). [Exploration in model-based reinforcement learning by empirically estimating learning progress](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2012/hash/a0a080f42e6f13b3a2df133f073095dd-Abstract.html). NeurIPS 25. Full PDF and publisher abstract: restricted assumptions, nonstationarity.
- **[F6]** Ecoffet, A., Huizinga, J., Lehman, J., Stanley, K. O., & Clune, J. (2021). [First return, then explore](https://arxiv.org/abs/2004.12919). *Nature*, 590, 580–586; author preprint v6 inspected in full. Same substantive work as original [S10].
- **[F7]** Lu, P.-Y., Cheng, Y.-J., Li, C.-L., & Lin, H.-T. (2025). [An expanded benchmark that rediscovers and affirms the edge of uncertainty sampling for active learning in tabular datasets](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.08954). *Transactions on Machine Learning Research*; **abstract and metadata only**. It motivates a strong baseline, not numerical claims or domain transfer.
