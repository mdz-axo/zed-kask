# Essentialist

General-purpose recursive eliminative interrogation. Enforces "always take away, never add" through a 3-gate challenge loop (Exist → Surface → Contract) that every artifact must survive before being committed. G1 and G2 delegate to deep-module's deletion test and surface assessment; G3 is this skill's own abstraction trace.

## Reference model and D/P labelling

Ousterhout, *A Philosophy of Software Design* (2018), Ch. 4 "Modules Should Be Deep" and Ch. 7.1's pass-through rule (`onto_anchor` → derived `deep_module`, operator ruling 2026-09-26): deletion losing no behavior is the shallow-module criterion; trivial inlining is the pass-through criterion. The loop adapts Fagan's inspection — detection separated from correction, rework verified in follow-up (derived `fagan_inspection`). The constraint-force vocabulary is pragmatic-semantics'; the one-gate loop control is grounded in MDL (Rissanen 1978 `doi:10.1016/0005-1098(78)90005-5` → derived `occam_razor`). Gate evaluation and reduction are P (the delegates, the agent, the human in advisory mode); mode resolution, gate order, retry and round bounds, zero-delta detection and the score are D — all in one `lisp_eval` form, `ESS-1`.

## Initial and target condition

- **Initial condition:** the artifact, its callers and dependencies (`code_context`), the user's request verbatim, and a caller-supplied `mode`.
- **Target condition:** `ESS-1` returns `complete` — all three gates passed in one round with a snapshot identical to the round's starting snapshot — or `escalate` with its reason; every reduction applied in advisory mode traces to a human `accept` or `override` recorded in the decisions passed to the gate.

## When to Use

- An artifact (module, function, trait, type, or interface) needs to be interrogated for unnecessary complexity, pass-through wrappers, or over-engineered abstractions.
- The user asks to simplify, strip or "run the essentialist" (autonomous mode), or wants reductions recommended and decided per item (advisory mode).
- A codebase has accumulated thin wrappers, single-use traits, or public-surface bloat that should be challenged before commit.

## When NOT to Use

- Additive work — the gates assume an artifact to reduce.
- A single code module where deep-module alone is wanted — essentialist is the general interrogator over it.
- Autonomous reduction without the user's explicit words — `ESS-1` resolves such a request to advisory.

## Instructions

Create the run directory `~/Documents/zk-data/skills/essentialist/{date}-{run}/` with `terminal`. Keep `state.json` there: it starts as `{}` and is replaced by the `state` object of every `ESS-1` result.

1. **Snapshot.** Run `deep-module/deep-module-assess` once with `module_path` = the artifact's path, `module_source` = its source, `caller_paths` = the caller paths from `code_context`, `codebase_context` = `code_context`. From its `interface_inventory` build the snapshot `{ "surviving_items": [<every counted public item's name>], "structure": [<module and sub-module names>], "interfaces": [<every public signature>] }`. The first snapshot's item count is the score's denominator. Re-run the assessment and rebuild the snapshot after every reduction; a snapshot the model edits by hand is not a snapshot.

2. **Evaluate the gate `ESS-1` names** (G1 first; after `next_gate` the next; after `reduce` or `next_round`, G1 again):
   - **G1 — EXIST.** Render `deep-module/deep-module-delete` with `module_assessment` = the whole assess output, `caller_code` and `dependency_code` from `code_context`, `codebase_context` = `code_context`. Its `recommendation` `DELETE` or `MERGE` is a finding on the artifact with `action` of the same name; `DEEPEN` is a `guideline` finding with action `MERGE` carried to G2; `EXTRACT` is no finding. The delegate's `recommendation_force` is the finding's `constraint_force`.
   - **G2 — SURFACE.** From the current assessment: each public item beyond the delegate's seven-function threshold without a written justification is a `guardrail` finding with action `MERGE` (or `JUSTIFY` when it must stay separate); the delegate's `red_flags` are `evidence` findings with action `RETAIN`. Challenge: what if the artifact had exactly one public function?
   - **G3 — CONTRACT.** Trace every abstraction boundary: a trait with one implementor, a wrapper or adapter adding no behavior beyond a direct call, a config struct passed through untouched, an error type wrapping exactly one inner error, a generic parameter with one concrete type. Each is a `prohibition` finding with action `DELETE`. G3 passes when every abstraction encodes behavior beyond a direct call.
   Every finding is `{ "item": <name>, "constraint_force": <prohibition|guardrail|guideline|evidence|hypothesis>, "action": <DELETE|MERGE|JUSTIFY|RETAIN>, "issue": <one sentence>, "recommendation": <the specific reduction> }`. Render `essentialist/essentialist-flow` once at the start for the gate definitions and the finding shape; it is a reference, not a per-gate call.

3. **Run `ESS-1`** with the request, the caller's mode, the gate just evaluated, its findings, the human's decisions (empty in autonomous mode or before the human has answered), the current snapshot, and `state.json`:

On `escalate`: the reason, the contested `items`, the log, and the decision the human must make. After `escalate`, STOP; the gate refuses further steps with `loop_closed`.

## Constraints

- Every finding carries `item`, `constraint_force` and `action` in the gate's vocabulary; a finding outside it makes the step `invalid`, never a silent pass.
- Only `prohibition` and `guardrail` findings reduce in autonomous mode; in advisory mode only the human's `accept` and reasoned `override` do. A prohibition rejected, or a guardrail rejected without a reason, escalates.
- Three reductions on one gate in a round, or three rounds that still change the artifact, escalate. After `escalate` or `complete` the loop is closed.
- The snapshot comes from the assessment, the counts from the gate: type no item list, count, retry number or score into the env or the report.
