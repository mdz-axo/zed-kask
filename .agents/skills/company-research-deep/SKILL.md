# Company Research — Deep Pipeline

Deep company research pipeline adapted from EFRA-AI (Replicant-Partners). A request naming a company starts a sourced background, overview and outlook report; the distinct analytical perspectives supply the substance, their observations are checked against the sources, and the report synthesizes them. MCP tool calls (company_transcript, web_search, scenario_build, and the four valuation tools required at THESIS — dcf_valuation, comparable_analysis, reverse_dcf, expectations_gap) are called directly where applicable; templates synthesize over actual outputs.

## Reference models

The pipeline is converted from EFRA-AI (Replicant-Partners), a project source. The frameworks it applies carry their own authors: Moore, Johnson & Kippola, *The Gorilla Game* (1998) for GORILLA; Wardley, *Wardley Maps* (2016) for the value-chain map (`wardley-mapper`); Simon, *Hidden Champions of the 21st Century* (2009) for SIMON; Rappaport & Mauboussin, *Expectations Investing* (2001) for the expectations gap (`onto_anchor` → derived `expectations_gap`). Derived anchors (operator ruling 2026-09-25): `gorilla_game`, `wardley_map`, `hidden_champion`. The three-pillar thesis structure is anchored on the Merchant Adventures (MAIA) corpus, "Technological Capability and Human Judgment": business franchise (how the business takes one dollar and turns it into two), management quality/culture (the right people to keep it growing and making money), and valuation as the gap between business potential and the expectations discounted in the current price. Derived anchor (operator ruling 2026-09-27): `investment_thesis`.

The evidence review is built on published verification practice (operator rulings 2026-09-27): ISA 500 (*Audit Evidence*) for assertion-orientation, the evidence reliability hierarchy and inquiry-is-not-corroboration; vouching (claim → source) and tracing (source → report) for direction of testing; ISA 320 for materiality-scoped checking; ISA 230 for the working-paper record; Heuer's analysis of competing hypotheses for disagreement adjudication; Caulfield's SIFT for trace-to-original collection; IEEE 1012 for integrity-level proportionality; W3C PROV for provenance shape.

**D/P labelling.** MCP valuation tools and fixed-weight `lisp_eval` GORILLA scoring are D. Board rotation, Wardley evolution, capability assessments, Simon inquiry, IMAGINE scenarios and thesis pillars are judgments grounded in cited evidence and open to contrary cases. The evidence review records what was checked and what was found; it never certifies the report, and no mechanical match upgrades a claim's provenance.

## When to Use

- When the user asks for a deep background, overview and outlook report on a company, using its name or ticker. No separately supplied research question, ticker, source list, acceptance task or thesis is required; derive the internal work from this request and the actual evidence. For a company without the inputs needed for an investment thesis, produce a sourced qualitative report with explicit gaps.
- When you want the full EFRA-AI research pipeline (COMPANY → GORILLA → IMAGINE → THESIS) as a single governed process.
- When you want the 8-part company analysis (Self-View, Business Franchise, Management Skill, Financial Profile, Invisible Layer, Falstaffian Inversion, Value Gorilla Elevator Pitch, Investment Thesis Statement).
- When you want the GORILLA 4-dimension framework (Obvious Problem / Invisible Gorilla / Combinatorial Solution / Choke Point) with fixed methodology weights.
- When you want IMAGINE's 5/10Y scenarios with digital transformation stage classification and falsifiable predictions persisted to the forecast-ledger.
- When you want the THESIS three-pillar synthesis (Business Franchise, Management Quality, Valuation) with its explicit price conclusion.

## When NOT to Use

- A quick take or initiation note — use `company-research-flash` (the flash pipeline).
- Live trading signals — the deliverable is a sourced report with a falsifiable prediction set, not an execution signal.
- An investment-grade verdict — that is a separate, stricter outcome this pipeline never claims.

## Instructions

Execution order: collect-evidence → LISTEN → COMPANY → INDUSTRY OUTSIDE VIEW → FALSTAFFIAN → WARDLEY → ECONOMIC TRAJECTORY → GORILLA and capability checks → SIMON → IMAGINE → value-gorilla answer → THESIS → PREPARE full report and summary → evidence-review → PERSIST both. Each named analytical template is a prompt scaffold over actual preceding evidence, not an inference call or an original source.

**Every step runs on every report.** No perspective is skipped, deferred or bypassed; a perspective that cannot establish its observations on a given company still runs and records what it could not establish and why — that limitation appears in the report, never as a silently missing section.

**Run mode and dependency waves.** At intake set `run_mode` to `one_shot` unless the operator requests `collaborative` (interactive); record the choice and fix `as_of_date` to the operator-supplied date, or the intake run date if none was supplied, for the entire run, including pauses and reruns. Both modes execute the same stages and gates below. A stage starts when its inputs exist, not when the previous line ends. Independent stages in a wave may run in parallel (one `spawn_agent` per independent stage if delegated); dependent steps within a wave finish as a chain. In `collaborative`, the primary agent owns the run and delivers each checkpoint in this conversation; never delegate the whole run to a subagent that cannot receive the operator's feedback. Do not advance a consumer on a rendered prompt, an unvalidated output or a merely scheduled task.

For **each** named template stage, pass the current source-keyed digest and predecessor outputs in the template's declared input fields; call `render_template`, then actually execute the rendered prompt as an inference task (directly or via a delegated agent), and inspect the returned result. Rendering checks template inputs only: it does **not** run inference or validate the model's output. Check the result against that template's declared output field names and types, the stage-specific fields required below, and each factual citation key against a held source record with matching period, unit and qualification. Check the exact output shape at the next consumer's input fields before handing it off (including nullable inputs and required `valuation_evidence`); a missing or wrong-shaped handoff is not a successful stage. On failure, name the stage, missing key/type/source or consumer mismatch, repair the input or re-execute the affected stage and its descendants, then check again. If it cannot be repaired, record the actual failure and `data_gaps`; never fabricate a stage result or silently advance a required consumer. Keep working stage state in this thread, not in run folders; record actual `run`, `skipped: <reason>`, failure, repair and rerun outcomes for the final `stages_run` manifest, without marking a failed execution as run. The existing source and valuation gates remain in force in either mode.

| Wave | Stages (parallel within a wave) | Consumes |
|------|--------------------------------|----------|
| 1 | collect-evidence (writes the evidence digest) ‖ INDUSTRY OUTSIDE VIEW searches and source retrieval | company identity |
| 2 | LISTEN | digest, sources |
| 3 | COMPANY; then render INDUSTRY OUTSIDE VIEW against the board | digest, `listening_view` |
| 4 | FALSTAFFIAN → WARDLEY → ECONOMIC TRAJECTORY (one agent, one chain) | board, outside view |
| 5 | GORILLA + capability checks → SIMON | rotated board, Wardley, trajectory, `listening_view` |
| 6 | IMAGINE → value-gorilla answer → THESIS | wave 5 outputs |
| 7 | PREPARE → evidence-review → PERSIST/CONDENSE | all drafts |

The outside-view searches query industry economics, never the company, so they need only the identity; the render step waits for the board's value chain. A delegated stage receives the digest and its predecessors' outputs, not the full documents. In `one_shot`, advance from each completed wave to the next without waiting for the operator; publish only after the existing evidence review and two-deliverable checks. In `collaborative`, after a **whole wave** finishes and its outputs pass the shared checks, pause before starting the next wave. Show a concise checkpoint with findings, best contrary evidence, unknowns, completed/failed stage status, and the next dependency; ask for context or `continue`. Never pause between steps of a dependent chain or midway through a wave. On `continue` without new evidence, resume the same run in this thread at the next wave, with the same as-of date, digest, validated outputs and source keys — do not restart or re-ask for intake. Operator comments are separate, uncorroborated provenance, not a citation or permission to override an original. If a comment changes a material claim, perform a targeted check against an independently retrieved, period- and definition-matched source; until then retain it as a disputed claim or named gap. When new checked evidence changes an input, update the digest, invalidate only affected stage outputs and rerun their dependent descendants with the same stage checks before advancing; preserve unrelated validated outputs and the original as-of date. After wave 7, the checkpoint is the reviewed deliverables, not a pause before publishing them.

**Extract once.** collect-evidence is the only stage that reads the sources in full. It compacts them once into a working evidence digest — every figure, statement line, quote and disclosure the later stages may use, each with its citation key, verbatim text, period, unit and provenance level, plus the computed Financial Signposts and Essential Statistics — and every later stage works from that digest and its own predecessors' outputs instead of re-reading the documents. A stage that needs a fact the digest lacks goes back to that source for a targeted extraction instead of a new broad search. The evidence review is the exception: it checks the report's claims against the actual sources. A request for a report on an identifiable company is the full intake: derive the default background (what it does and how it developed), current overview (business, management, finances when available, competitors and risks), and evidence-qualified outlook (drivers, scenarios and what would change the view). Use the operator-supplied as-of date when present (otherwise the intake run date) and derive narrow internal research questions from the report sections; do not ask the user to write those questions or choose a test company. When equity-only tools or audited financial inputs do not apply, do not fabricate ticker/price/DCF inputs, bind missing values to zero, or run a numerical investment gate on qualitative proxies. Record each unavailable stage in `data_gaps`, continue the source-grounded business/risk/outlook sections that can be supported, and run the evidence review over the report actually written. The report carries `incomplete` or `not_applicable` for unsupported elements; the user still receives the requested company background/overview/outlook as a labelled draft rather than a made-up valuation.

**Synthesis, not concatenation.** The report is written analysis, not pasted stage outputs. Each perspective's section states its company-specific conclusion, the mechanism behind it, its best opposing case, and what it means for the outlook. Where two perspectives disagree, the report says so and settles it with evidence — which position the sources support better and why — or states plainly what evidence would resolve it. A disagreement smoothed over or silently dropped is a defect the evidence review flags.

### collect-evidence

1. Before COMPANY, identify the company from the user's report request. If a public security is applicable, use `resolve_symbol` with the supplied name or ticker; do not require the user to provide a ticker. If no symbol exists (for example a private company), use its verified official identity for public-source discovery, omit ticker-only tools and mark their unavailable financial/market inputs as gaps. If the name refers to more than one plausible entity and sources cannot disambiguate it, ask only which company was intended; do not ask for an additional research question. Read `forecast_list` and relevant `report_load` only for an applicable resolved symbol, as prior analysis (not primary evidence).
2. Start with how the company sees itself: its own filings, reports and presentations, not sell-side or media reports (MAIA Guidebook, Company Analysis: Where to Start). Retrieve the company description/IR page, the relevant current and prior earnings transcripts, and the historical financial inputs needed by the analysis. Use `company_profile`, `company_transcript`, `income_statement`, `balance_sheet`, `cash_flow_statement`, and `stock_quote` as applicable; fetch the cited filing or original page with `web_extract` before treating a search snippet as support for a load-bearing claim. Keep provider warnings, financial periods, units, and audit status. A search that returns no result is not evidence of market absence.
3. Collect evidence for meaning, not form. Keep a document only if it bears on the business, management, valuation, risks or thesis, the growth and profitability an owner weighs (Guidebook, Thinking Like an Owner: growth and profitability, "not technical trading patterns or any of the hundreds of other distractions the market provides"). Read the company's own statements for the Financial Signposts first: gross margin stability through a cycle, days payables minus days sales outstanding, the CEO's record of returns on capital against changes in invested capital over time, the CFO's working-capital consistency, and the essential statistics (Guidebook, Financial Signposts and Essential Statistics). Boilerplate, meaning text with form but no bearing on the company's economics (routine third-party ownership threshold notices, legal notices, contact pages, results about other entities), is not evidence: do not retrieve, list, classify or count it, and its absence is never a gap or `not_checked`. An agent that spends the run processing boilerplate instead of the company's economics has failed the skill; its output is discarded and not retried. For each source you use, hold its citation identity: URL, document title, retrieval date, period, units, audit status, and the retrieved text; give it a short citation key the report will cite. Keep original passages separate from summaries, and record failed retrievals as named gaps. When a valuation, signpost or quote-bearing tool's calculation reaches the report, keep its actual response and distinguish it from audited input; a model-generated stage output is labelled as such, never an original. Neither tool transport nor a citation list upgrades a summary to original evidence.
4. Compact the evidence once (see Extract once) and pass the digest records to `company-research/company-8part` for synthesis alongside its existing inputs. Maintain the digest throughout all later research, including Wardley and the literature probe. Every factual claim's citation must resolve to a source you actually hold. Missing evidence remains a named gap, not an LLM estimate.
5. The report is the only durable artifact: no run folders, no retained-output files, no intermediate evidence files. Sources, digest and stage outputs are working state for this report.

### listen

Read the company the way a long-term owner listens: once, through the `listening` skill's horizon model, so every later stage works from the same cited strategic evidence instead of re-reading raw documents and chasing hype or short-term noise.

1. After collect-evidence, split the company's own narrative originals into verbatim passages of at most ~800 words at paragraph or speaker-turn boundaries: the current and prior earnings-call transcripts, the annual report's business and MD&A sections, and any investor-day or shareholder-letter material retained. Each passage is `{source, entity_ref, text}` with `source` = its citation key. Do not pass financial statement tables, notes or boilerplate. A missing prior call is a `data_gaps` entry (management_consistency then rests on the current documents only).
2. Follow the `listening` skill process with `listening/apply-template-rag` (`corpus_passages`, `kg_triples: []` unless a company graph exists, `company_symbol`, `focus_query` = the report's background/overview/outlook scope). Verify every cited quote mechanically against its own passage. First test `lisp_eval` `string-contains` with a known present and known absent substring. If it fails that control or disagrees with an independent byte-exact check, use an exact-match check over the original keyed passage (e.g. POSIX `grep -F` with literal fixed text supplied safely as a file, not a shell-interpolated expression); disclose the substitution and record the control results. Drop a quote that fails twice. Record `(list matched total dropped)` in `listening_view` as mechanical counts, not a semantic verdict.
3. Emit `listening_view` (per-section verdicts against the company's own stated strategy, `checkpoint_map`, `strategic_goals`, `ignored_short_term`, counts, `data_gaps`). COMPANY, GORILLA capability checks, IMAGINE, THESIS and prepare-summary consume it instead of the raw narrative text: Self-View and Management Skill quote its verified evidence; the execution-evidence and multi-quarter customer checks use `management_consistency` and the checkpoint map; IMAGINE's predictions and the summary's Future section start from the checkpoint map. Consult an original passage directly only to check a specific quote or fill a named gap. No verdict, score or forecast input may come from `ignored_short_term`. Hype with no dated checkpoint, delivered result or audited support stays context, never corroboration.
4. Passages and the listening view are working state for the report. Before quoting a passage, check that the exact wording appears in its keyed source; the evidence review's mechanical counts record the quote checks for the composed texts.

### Data Provenance Hierarchy (applies to all steps)

All financial data used in the pipeline must be classified by provenance strength. Stronger provenance overrides weaker provenance when they conflict.

| Level | Source | Legal standing | Use as DCF/valuation input? |
|-------|--------|---------------|---------------------------|
| 1 | Audited annual financial statements (in a 10-K or equivalent filing) | Auditor's opinion applies to the financial statements, not every statement in the filing | **Yes — these are the historical anchor** |
| Interim | Financial statements in a 10-Q | Filed, but unaudited interim statements | Cross-check/context; do not label as audited historical inputs |
| 2 | Earnings call transcripts | Regulation FD applies, but forward-looking statements have safe harbor | No — use for qualitative analysis, management assessment, milestone tracking |
| 3 | Press releases / investor presentations | Selective disclosure, not audited | No — use for context, not for financial inputs |
| 4 | Media coverage / analyst reports | Third-party interpretation, may repeat company claims | No — use for incumbent research, market context |
| 5 | Web research / news | Variable quality | No — use for market-reality gate, incumbent verification |

**Historical DCF inputs must come from Level 1 (audited financial statements) only.** Forecast growth, margins and discount rates are explicit assumptions, not audited facts; preserve their rationale and distinguish tool calculations from evidence validating those assumptions. If audited data is insufficient for a meaningful DCF — e.g., pre-revenue company with no historical revenue, margins, or working capital data — the correct output is "insufficient audited data for DCF," not substituting analyst estimates, round-number guesses, or transcript claims as inputs. Swapping in non-audited data as DCF inputs pollutes the calculation and produces a number that has the appearance of rigor without the substance.

**Customer base evidence** must distinguish between:
- Revenue from paying customers (Level 1 — from audited financials)
- Customer/partner counts cited in transcripts (Level 2 — management claim, not audited)
- Partnership announcements in press releases (Level 3 — selective disclosure)

A company citing "60+ MNO partners covering 3B+ subscribers" in a transcript (Level 2) does not constitute evidence of paying customers. Recognized revenue in audited financials supports that sales occurred; it does not verify that every named partner pays, a claimed customer count, retention, or product-level unit economics. Those claims each require their own supporting disclosure.

### company-8part

1. Self-View — how the company describes itself (verbatim, from `listening_view`'s verified evidence and the fetched IR page).
2. Business Franchise — identity, geography, moat, competitive position.
3. Management Skill — CEO scorecard (long-term) + CFO scorecard (working capital) with verbatim transcript quotes.
4. Financial Profile — signposts (the valuation synthesis belongs to THESIS, which consumes the valuation tool outputs directly).
5. Invisible Layer — what's not on the page, what's not in the price.
6. Falstaffian Inversion — pierce market ceremony and social illusion (honour is "air"); find economic life where the consensus disdains to look (the "spare men"); early shoots of improvement the consensus misses.
7. Value Gorilla Elevator Pitch — the economic opportunity, what the company is doing to exploit it, and why it is likely to succeed while the market prices it as if it will not (Guidebook, Value Gorillas), stated only as far as verified evidence supports.
8. Investment Thesis Statement — state whether evidence supports a thesis. If insufficient, say so.
9. Consume company_transcript, web_search, fetch MCP tool outputs.
10. Emit data_gaps for any failed MCP tool — never collapse to None.
11. **Intent-routed search:** Call `web_search` with the `intent` hint (news, academic, semantic, freshness, general, transcript) and no explicit `provider` — the tool scores the configured providers against (query, intent) and queries the top recommendation as a single-provider call, surfacing the ranking in `provider_recommendations` and the choice in `selected_provider`. This prevents the arxiv-only fallback that occurs when no provider is explicitly selected and the credential map is stale.
12. **Earnings call transcripts:** If `company_transcript` is unavailable, search for `"{company} earnings call transcript Q{N} {year}"` via `web_search(provider="serpapi")` — SerpAPI supports YouTube transcript extraction. Fall back to `web_search(provider="tavily")` for written transcript sources. Never substitute Wikipedia-sourced paraphrases for verbatim management quotes without flagging a `data_gap`.

### industry-outside-view

An advisory outside view on the industry's business drivers, built from scholarly and institutional sources rather than web opinion. Its searches and retrieval run in wave 1 alongside collect-evidence; its synthesis renders after COMPANY (which anchors on how the company sees itself) and before FALSTAFFIAN. It never blocks and never scores.

1. Name the industry from the company's own self-description (profile and IR page) for the searches; the render step checks it against the COMPANY board's value chain. Query the industry's economics, never the company's name: e.g. `"{industry} cost structure capital intensity pricing power"`, `"{industry} cyclicality entry barriers competition"`, `"{industry} industrial organization market structure"`.
2. Search these tiers, each with explicit `web_search(provider=...)` calls, and log every call's query, provider, `count` and `providers_failed`:
   - Scholarly: `openalex`, `arxiv`, `semantic_scholar` (free) and `google_scholar` (SerpAPI key). Resolve each candidate with `resolve_paper`; an unresolvable identity is excluded. Prefer systematic reviews, meta-analyses and industrial-organization or strategy journals; Google Scholar's `[cited by N]` indicates uptake, not truth.
   - Business-school cases and faculty research: `web_search(provider="serpapi", include_domains=["hbsp.harvard.edu", "thecasecentre.org", "iveypublishing.ca", "gsb.stanford.edu"])` for case catalogues, and `["hbs.edu", "knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu", "insead.edu", "sloanreview.mit.edu"]` for faculty research. A catalogue or author listing is a lead; a case counts only when its own abstract page is retrieved.
   - Books: `google_books` (SerpAPI key). Keep academic or established trade publishers; cite without quoting unless the text itself is retrieved.
   - Industry self-regulatory organizations and standard-setting bodies: search for the industry's SROs, standards bodies and statistical associations (e.g. `"{industry} self-regulatory organization"`, `"{industry} industry standards body"`), then `web_extract` their own published standards, rules or statistics.
   - Substack: admissible only when the author is identified and the retrieved publication page shows more than 1,000 subscribers.
3. Never search Reddit, forums or general web results for this step, and never use answer boxes. Exclude consultant and industry-association reports unless they are in an admissible tier or cited by a scholarly, case or book source. Apply the boilerplate rule: a source with no bearing on this industry's economics is dropped without being listed or counted.
4. Pass short keyed extracts as `source_evidence` plus the call log as `search_log` to `company-research/industry-outside-view`, with `ticker`, `industry` and `company_board`. A failed or empty tier is a `data_gaps` entry, not a block.
5. Before passing the output on, check its field names with `lisp_eval`, binding `view` to the returned JSON object:

   ```lisp
   (let ((has (lambda (k entries) (if (is_null entries) false (if (string= k (car (car entries))) true (has k (cdr entries)))))) (missing (lambda (keys) (if (is_null keys) (list) (if (has (car keys) view) (missing (cdr keys)) (cons (car keys) (missing (cdr keys)))))))) (missing (list "industry_drivers" "self_regulatory_bodies" "company_fit" "frame_conflicts" "implication_for_falstaffian" "implication_for_gorilla" "implication_for_thesis" "claim_sources" "excluded_sources" "data_gaps")))
   ```

   A non-empty result lists missing fields: re-render once with that list; if fields are still missing, pass the view with the gap recorded in `data_gaps`. It never blocks the pipeline.
6. Emit `industry_outside_view`. Pass it to FALSTAFFIAN (frame conflicts), GORILLA (Obvious Problem and Choke Point) and THESIS (Business Franchise and Risks); its frame conflicts appear in the report and are settled in the synthesis.

### falstaffian-competitive-rotation

1. Rotate the competitive framing of the Company Board before GORILLA scores it — structural defense against frame capture by analyst narratives. Render `company-research/falstaffian-competitive-rotation` with `industry_outside_view` (or null) so outside-source frame conflicts are candidate framing errors.
2. Apply Falstaffian semantic rotation shapes (predicate hollow, subject expansion, object inversion, direction reversal) to expose framing errors.
3. Emit rotated_board with competitor-complement analysis, market creator vs participant classification, framing errors detected, and rotated competitive position.
4. GORILLA consumes the rotated board, not the raw Company Board.

### wardley-anchor

1. Compressed value-chain map of the rotated Company Board — compresses wardley-mapper's 6-step process into a single LLM call.
2. **Inventory two classes of components:**
   - **Internal value-chain components** — what the company builds. Classify each on the Wardley evolution axis (Genesis → Custom → Product → Commodity).
   - **Claimed market categories** — every market or capability the company claims to own, create, or be unique in. For each, search via `web_search` for incumbents who already sell it as a product line. Classify based on **what incumbents do**, not what the company claims. If incumbents sell it as a product line, it is at Product/Commodity stage — flag it as a commoditization red flag.
3. Surface commoditization candidates (including any claimed market found to be at Product/Commodity stage), choke points, and invisible gorillas.
4. GORILLA consumes choke_points, invisible_gorillas, AND commoditization_candidates — a claimed market at Product/Commodity stage caps the Obvious Problem score (the company is entering an existing market, not solving a new problem). ECONOMIC TRAJECTORY consumes commoditization_candidates as the falling-cost anchor.
5. **Conditional full Wardley:** If the compressed choke_point score (from GORILLA, evaluated after this step) is < 60, re-run with the full `wardley-mapper` skill (6-step process: inventory → classify → map → movement → recommendations → present). The full map adds movement analysis (component velocity on the evolution axis) and visual positioning that the compressed adapter cannot produce. This conditional trigger avoids convergence fatigue on clear-cut cases while preserving depth when the strategic position is uncertain.

### economic-trajectory

Anchored on the Merchant Adventures (MAIA) posts: "Focus and Imagination" (imagination anchored in reality), "More From Less" (McAfee — dematerialization, the falling cost), "Bottlenecks and Critical Mass" (constraint removal), "Stuart A. Kauffman Readings" (adjacent possible, economic web, preadaptations), "The Future Is Faster Than You Think" (Diamandis — convergence), "Time Horizons" (the walk-back method).

1. Identify the falling-cost trajectory in the subject's industry, the design constraint being removed, the Coasean firm-boundary shifts, and the Kauffman adjacent possible nodes.
2. Consume the Wardley map's commoditization_candidates as the falling-cost anchor — what's becoming commodity IS the falling-cost trajectory.
3. **Strategy-literature-probe:** Before asserting Coasean shifts or adjacent-possible nodes, search for grounding literature via `web_search(provider="exa")` with semantically-framed queries: `"industrial organization market structure entry barriers {industry}"`, `"case study vertical integration {industry} {strategy}"`, `"platform economics two-sided market {business_model}"`. Exa's neural search finds literature by meaning, not keyword. Consume the results as evidence for the trajectory claims — do not assert theoretical grounding without a source. Emit `literature_sources` with each trajectory claim.
4. Emit economic_trajectory with falling_cost, constraint_being_removed, coasean_shifts, adjacent_possible_nodes, convergence_vectors, implications_for_subject, trajectory_velocity (rapid | moderate | slow — how fast the falling cost moves), literature_sources.
5. IMAGINE consumes this as the anchor for its 5/10Y scenarios.

### gorilla-4dim

1. **Market-reality gate.** For each market the company claims to own or have created, search via `web_search` for incumbents who sell it as a product line. Output: `claimed market | incumbent | what they sell | URL`. If incumbents exist, cap Obvious Problem at 40. If no incumbents found after searching, state this explicitly and score normally.
2. **Financial red-flag screen.** Answer two questions using Level 1 (audited financial statement) data only:
   - Is capex producing revenue? (Compare capex and revenue trajectories from audited annual statements; use unaudited 10-Q figures only as explicitly labelled interim cross-checks.)
   - Is revenue real? (Check whether reported revenue is from paying customers or from contracts/MoUs. Audited revenue is recognized revenue — not backlog, not contracted, not "committed.")
   If audited data is insufficient to answer either question, state "insufficient audited data" and apply a confidence penalty. If either answer is negative based on audited data, apply a confidence penalty to all GORILLA scores.
3. **Falsification before scoring above 70.** Write one paragraph arguing against any dimension scored above 70. If the counter-argument is more convincing than the supporting evidence, reduce the score.
4. Score the 4 dimensions (Obvious Problem, Invisible Gorilla, Combinatorial Solution, Choke Point) against the ROTATED Company Board, the Wardley map, the economic trajectory, the market-reality gate, and the financial red-flag screen.
5. Every score must cite evidence: transcript quote, web source URL, or financial data point. A score without cited evidence is invalid.
6. Call `lisp_eval` to apply fixed weights (25/30/25/20) and compute the verdict (GORILLA ≥75 / SMALL_ANIMAL 50-74 / PEDESTRIAN <50).
   Pinned form: `(let ((score (+ (* 0.25 obvious_problem) (* 0.30 invisible_gorilla) (* 0.25 combinatorial_solution) (* 0.20 choke_point)))) (cond ((>= score 75) 'GORILLA) ((>= score 50) 'SMALL_ANIMAL) (t 'PEDESTRIAN)))` — env binds each dimension's 0–100 score; maturity-blocked dimensions (from gorilla-capability-reason) bind to 0, not their elicited value.
7. Do NOT propose alternative weightings.

### gorilla-capability-reason

1. Type each GORILLA dimension against a capability registry with floor, ceiling, and maturity-gate limits.
2. **Execution evidence requirement.** For each dimension scored above 50, cite one prior-quarter guidance item or stated milestone from the Company Board and whether it was delivered. The comparison is: `prior guidance | delivered? | evidence`. If delivery is absent across 2+ dimensions, apply a maturity block — the dimension contributes 0 to the fixed-weight score, not its elicited value; retain the explicit maturity block and evidence gap separately. This distinguishes companies that see an opportunity and are executing from companies that see an opportunity and are not.
3. **Customer base evidence requirement.** The most valuable asset any business has is its paying customer base (Reichheld: loyal, low-turnover customers drive ROIC by reducing acquisition costs and enabling referral-driven growth). For each dimension scored above 50, cite evidence of paying customers using the Data Provenance Hierarchy:
   - **Paying customers (Level 1 — audited)**: recognized revenue from audited annual statements. Customer counts need their own disclosure and audit-status check; location in a filing alone does not make a count audited. Calculate ARPU only from definition- and period-aligned sourced revenue and counts. Unaudited 10-Q figures remain labelled interim context.
   - **Non-paying relationships (Level 2-3 — unaudited)**: partnerships, MoUs, letters of intent, "agreements" cited in transcripts or press releases that have not converted to recognized revenue
   If the company has no Level 1 evidence of paying customers but claims a large partner ecosystem (Level 2-3), apply a maturity block on Obvious Problem and Combinatorial Solution. A company that cannot get people to pay for its product has not demonstrated the capability to monetize the opportunity, regardless of opportunity size.
   Cross-reference `listening_view` (from LISTEN: `management_consistency` and the checkpoint map) for multi-quarter trajectory: are customer counts growing? Are partners converting to paying customers? Is revenue per customer growing? Persistent partner counts without revenue conversion is a signal that the company sees the opportunity but cannot execute on the most basic business function.
4. Evaluate whether the GORILLA scores are defensible given the company's capability maturity, execution track record, and customer base evidence.
5. Emit floor_violations, ceiling_violations, maturity_blocks. Finalize the fixed-weight GORILLA score only after these checks; earlier scores are provisional. A maturity block contributes 0 for that dimension in the `lisp_eval` scoring call; it remains an explicit block in the assessment, not a zero-valued measurement.

### hidden-champions-inquiry

1. After GORILLA's capability check, render `company-research/hidden-champions-inquiry` with `ticker`, `rotated_board`, `wardley_map`, `source_evidence` and `demand_context` (null unless a dated, category-matched demand source is retained). Construct `source_evidence` as short extracts keyed by citation key with URL, period and unit; the full sources stay in the working evidence.
2. Use Hermann Simon's *Hidden Champions of the 21st Century* as a flexible inquiry into the customer's need, narrow market boundary, focus, deep solution, customer proximity, innovation, globalization and the tradeoff between niche leadership and market saturation. These are observed patterns, **not** a required eight-item checklist, revenue limit, score or investment gate. The existing fixed-weight GORILLA verdict remains untouched.
3. Emit `simon_inquiry`: a source-keyed niche definition, `supported | contested | unknown` **leadership claim**, mechanism and contrary evidence, concentration and growth options, exceptions, implications for scenarios/thesis, and data gaps. A share requires compatible numerator, denominator, geography and period; management's "number one" alone is not verification. Distinguish company integrity and price expectations from this strategic lens. Missing source evidence yields `unknown`, never a favorable classification or automatic exclusion.
4. Pass `simon_inquiry` (or explicit null with the named failure in `data_gaps`) to `company-research/imagine-longrange` and `company-research/thesis-three-pillars`. Pass `industry_outside_view` (or null) to `company-research/gorilla-4dim` and `company-research/thesis-three-pillars` the same way. Reconcile it with Wardley and Falstaffian findings; contradictions remain visible in the report and are settled in the synthesis.

### imagine-longrange

1. Classify the digital transformation stage (MODEL / SHADOW / TWIN / SOURCE).
2. Classify the growth driver (innovation / demographic / both / neither).
3. Construct 5/10Y scenarios using the scenario_build MCP tool output as scaffold, ANCHORED on the economic trajectory and CHALLENGED by the Falstaffian rotations. When `simon_inquiry` is present, test whether niche saturation, adjacent demand or disputed leadership changes a scenario; do not assume a company fits every Simon trait.
4. Emit 3–5 falsifiable predictions tagged by horizon (5Y/10Y) for the forecast-ledger — the IMAGINE kata loop. Each prediction includes its own rationale and citation-key references, separate from the board-level rationale. An unsupported rationale records a `data_gaps` entry rather than an invented base rate.
5. Emit what's not on the page (anchored on the adjacent possible) and what's not in the price (anchored on the trajectory's implications).

### thesis-three-pillars

1. **Before rendering THESIS**, explicitly answer: is this a value gorilla at the current, as-of-dated price, and why or why not? Record `value_gorilla_judgment` as a source-keyed answer (`supported | contested | undetermined`, reasons and contrary evidence), using the final GORILLA capability result, paying-customer evidence, market price and IMAGINE 10Y scenario. If a price or necessary evidence is missing, say `undetermined`; never invent it. This is a pre-draft investment question, not a quality verdict. Pass the answer and the run's `as_of_date` to `company-research/thesis-three-pillars`.
2. **Assemble `valuation_evidence` before rendering** — the THESIS template's contract requires it, so the render fails closed without it. Run the four valuation tools directly if they have not run this report — `dcf_valuation`, `comparable_analysis`, `reverse_dcf`, `expectations_gap` — and assemble their outputs as `valuation_evidence: {dcf_result, comparables_result, reverse_dcf_result, expectations_gap_result}`. If a tool genuinely cannot run (it failed, or it is out of scope for the subject), record `valuation_evidence: {skip: {reason: "<the tool and why>"}}` — an explicit skip, never silence. The Valuation pillar always runs; a skipped valuation stage is carried through the report as `stage skipped (flagged)`, never as a clean result.
3. Synthesize the three-pillar investment thesis (Merchant Adventures reference model): Business/Franchise — what the company does to make money and why it is profitable and growing; Management — why the people running the company are the right ones to keep it growing and making money; Valuation — what expectations the current price discounts and the gap versus what the business is capable of achieving with quality management or the management it has. Consume `simon_inquiry` as advisory evidence about a customer-defined niche and its exceptions, not a hidden-champion verdict; unsupported share claims stay unknown. State an explicit price conclusion at the as-of price (attractive, unattractive or indeterminate and why), reconciled with IMAGINE 10Y and the gap assessment; a numeric price target is not required. Do not silently resolve an inconsistency between price and the 10Y scenario.
4. Keep the thesis (all three pillars, statement and rationale) in the time-relative present: qualify current claims by the as-of date; keep dated forecasts, management guidance and add/trim price levels in the report's Outlook section only, not in THESIS (IMAGINE is an internal input to Outlook). Do not copy future events into the ownership thesis.
5. **Before accepting THESIS**, apply the essentialist Exist test to each pillar: delete the pillar — does the thesis collapse? A pillar whose removal changes nothing is decorative; fold it in or cut it. Then check the thesis fields for dated forecasts, management guidance and add/trim price levels — they belong in the report's Outlook section only. Inspect and remove any actual forbidden content, check the as-of qualification and the three-pillar statement by reading the text, and repeat the check after any edit.
6. State whether the accumulated evidence supports a thesis, contradicts it, or is insufficient — and say so plainly. Do not assign a quality verdict; an investment-grade judgment is a separate outcome this pipeline never claims.

### prepare-summary

1. Draft the full report from the nine perspectives' observations: each perspective's company-specific conclusion with its mechanism, its best opposing case, and its implication for the outlook; settle disagreements between perspectives with evidence — which position the sources support better and why — or state plainly what evidence would resolve it. Include financial signposts and valuation only where historical audited inputs and modelled assumptions support them. If THESIS cannot support a price conclusion, state the valuation gap without inventing a target or dropping the other perspectives.
2. The report carries a **stages_run manifest**: one line per stage — the nine perspectives, the valuation tools, and the evidence review — marked `run` or `skipped: <reason>` from the actual stage record, including limitations and any failure/repair/rerun history. A stage with a validated output and a named limitation counts as `run` with that limitation stated; a failed execution is not `run`. The manifest is the report's own record of what happened; a missing manifest is a gap the evidence review flags.
3. Draft the MAIA team summary from the same sources as the full report: Name, Website, As of Date, sourced market fields if applicable, Business, Management, Valuation or an unavailable-data gap, Risks and Future. Keep it under 1,000 words; the summary is a shorter argument, not another pile of disclaimers. The Future section covers evidence-qualified developments to watch over the next 18 months, not a guaranteed forecast. Place forecast checkpoints and management guidance in Future/Outlook, not as timeless thesis facts. Every material sourced factual statement in both carries its original URL or citation key; a model judgment is labelled as such.
4. Compose both texts in full before any checking; the evidence review runs over exactly these composed texts, and persist writes them unchanged.

### evidence-review

Before the report is persisted, one author-side pass checks it against the sources, in the working-paper discipline of a financial audit review (ISA 500 assertion-orientation, vouching and tracing, ISA 320 materiality, ISA 230 documentation). It is one pass: corrections happen inside it; anything unresolved ships as a visible gap in the report.

1. Render `company-research/evidence-review` with the composed report and summary texts, the evidence digest (the working source records, each with its citation key, verbatim text, period, unit and provenance level) and the as-of date. Extract the load-bearing claims — those the report's conclusions would change if wrong — with the reason each is load-bearing. Incidental phrasing is not checked; the review's cost stays proportional to what matters.
2. Vouch each claim against the evidence: right period, right unit, right magnitude, right qualification, with the supporting source and passage named. A load-bearing claim backed only by a management statement is labelled uncorroborated and the report must say so; a claim supported by a single low-reliability source records a sufficiency note. A paraphrased claim with faithful period, unit, magnitude and qualification is as good as a quote — the review never rewards excerpt-style writing.
3. Trace each source for material facts the report should engage but does not; a found omission is added to the report or carried as a visible gap. This completeness direction runs even when every present claim is supported.
4. Check disagreement coverage: where the report's perspective sections disagree, the report must say so and settle it with evidence — which position the sources support better and why — or state plainly what evidence would resolve it. A disagreement smoothed over or silently dropped is a finding.
5. Run the mechanical checks — quoted phrases verbatim against their keyed originals, figures against their sources, and the stages_run manifest present with every stage marked run or skipped-with-reason (`lisp_eval`) — and record them as counts. A mechanical match is a count, never a verdict.
6. Summarize the review in the report itself: the claims checked with their results and provenance, the omissions found, the disagreements settled or deferred, the mechanical counts, and what was NOT checked and why. An unperformed check is a recorded limitation, never a clean result.

The report's label states exactly what was checked and what was not — never the word "verified". A report whose review note is absent says "no evidence review performed". The label distinguishes "stage run" from "stage skipped (flagged)"; a skipped stage never reads as a clean result.

### persist-report

1. After the evidence review, persist the exact reviewed full report and summary texts as **rich markdown files**, preserving the actual review outcome. Include the review note (claims checked, uncorroborated items, omissions, unresolved disagreements), the stages_run manifest, and the report's label. Do not rewrite factual content while publishing.
2. Reports are stored in the user-facing artifacts directory: `~/Documents/zk-data/companies-mcp/reports/`. This is separate from the internal data dir (`~/.local/share/zed-kask/`) — reports are user-facing artifacts that should be visible, not buried in a hidden cache directory.
3. Create the reports directory if it does not exist: `mkdir -p ~/Documents/zk-data/companies-mcp/reports` via `terminal`.
4. Write the markdown file to `~/Documents/zk-data/companies-mcp/reports/{ticker-or-company-slug}-{date}.md` for single-company analyses, or the corresponding two-identifier path for comparisons. Use a resolved ticker when one exists; otherwise derive the path-safe slug from the verified company name, never invent a listing. Use a quoted `terminal` heredoc for this external artifacts path; project file tools cannot write outside the workspace.
5. The markdown file is the deliverable — full rich markdown with all sections, mermaid diagrams, source notes, and citations.
6. Do NOT write reports to the source tree (`zed-kask/reports/` or similar) — that pollutes the user's code repository.
7. Do NOT write reports to the hidden internal data dir (`~/.local/share/zed-kask/mcp/companies/reports/`) — that buries user-facing output where the user will never find it.
8. Clean up (storage Cleanup rule). A revised report replaces the earlier file for the same company and date: write the new version at the same path rather than a `-v2` beside it. Delete working drafts, scratch evidence and a superseded version once the new report is persisted; keep an evidence folder only when the report cites files in it, and name it in the run summary. Never delete another company's or another date's report.

### condense-report

1. The full markdown report and the MAIA team write-up prepared in `prepare-summary` are the two deliverables. This step only exports the summary to `~/Documents/zk-data/companies-mcp/reports/{ticker-or-company-slug}-summary-{date}.md`, without composing new prose after the evidence review.
2. Both files live in `~/Documents/zk-data/companies-mcp/reports/` and carry the same review status. If either differs factually from the reviewed text, return it to the evidence review before release; otherwise label it with the same review status, never verified.

## Convergence

The pipeline converges when every perspective has run and its observations are in the report or its gaps; the load-bearing claims are checked against the sources; every disagreement between perspectives is settled with evidence or explicitly deferred; and the report carries its review note with its limitations. The report ships with its label stating what was checked and what was not. There is no quality verdict and no iteration budget: corrections happen inside the single evidence-review pass, and anything unresolved is a visible gap, not a reason to loop.

## Cross-Skill Composition

- LISTEN reuses the `listening` skill (`listening/apply-template-rag`) over the company's own narrative documents once, after collect-evidence; its verified `listening_view` replaces raw-narrative reading downstream.
- FALSTAFFIAN reuses the falstaffian perspective-engine shapes and decision tree (via `company-research/falstaffian-competitive-rotation`).
- WARDLEY compresses `wardley-mapper`'s 6-step process via the `company-research/wardley-anchor` adapter. Conditionally upgrades to full `wardley-mapper` when choke_point score < 60.
- ECONOMIC TRAJECTORY includes a strategy-literature-probe via Exa semantic search for IO/competition economics grounding.
- GORILLA runs `company-research/gorilla-capability-reason` — types each GORILLA dimension against a capability registry with floor/ceiling/maturity-gate limits. Dimensions with explicit maturity blocks contribute 0 in the fixed-weight `lisp_eval` score; missing source data remains a surfaced gap.
- SIMON renders `company-research/hidden-champions-inquiry` as an advisory Simon lens over the rotated company framing and Wardley map; its source-keyed output reaches IMAGINE and THESIS without modifying GORILLA weights.
- THESIS applies the essentialist Exist test to its own pillars as a drafting rule (delete the pillar — does the thesis collapse?); no separate gate renders.
- EVIDENCE-REVIEW renders `company-research/evidence-review` over the composed report texts — an author-side check in the audit working-paper discipline (ISA 500 assertion-orientation, vouching/tracing, ISA 320 materiality, ISA 230 documentation). Its summary goes in the report as the review note; a report without one is labelled "no evidence review performed". Its mechanical counts are never verdicts and its label never says verified.
- PERSIST/CONDENSE export those unchanged drafts (or explicitly labelled incomplete drafts) to `~/Documents/zk-data/companies-mcp/reports/` via `terminal`.

## MCP Tool Integration

All MCP tool calls are called directly (deterministic, governed, testable). See `kask/docs/architecture/skills-and-composition.md` Part II for the invocation patterns. Failed MCP tools surface as `data_gaps` entries in the consuming template — never collapse to None (per .rules).

## Constraints

- This SKILL.md body is the authoritative methodology. Jinja2 templates in the registry are structured reference versions of the same content.
- Every perspective runs on every report; none is skipped, deferred or bypassed. A perspective that cannot establish its observations records its limitation in the report.
- Every report carries a stages_run manifest listing each stage as run or skipped-with-reason; a skipped stage is labelled "stage skipped (flagged)" and never reads as a clean result.
- MCP tool failures must not collapse to None. Templates emit `data_gaps` entries naming the failed tool.
- The evidence review is one author-side pass over the composed report texts. Its summary is the report's review note; a report without one is labelled "no evidence review performed". Its label never says verified, and a mechanical match is a count, never a verdict.
- Historical DCF inputs require audited annual financial statements (Level 1 only). Forecast assumptions are labelled assumptions; a tool calculation is not evidence validating its own inputs.
- A claim only management has made is labelled as management's claim; inquiry is not corroboration (ISA 500).
- The report is written synthesis: each perspective's conclusion with its best opposing case, disagreements settled with evidence or explicitly deferred. Pasted stage outputs and boilerplate risk lists are defects.
- IMAGINE's falsifiable predictions are persisted to the forecast-ledger (when built — Phase 3) for later Brier scoring.
- No investment-grade verdict: an investment-grade judgment is a separate outcome this pipeline never claims. State whether evidence supports a thesis, contradicts it, or is insufficient.
